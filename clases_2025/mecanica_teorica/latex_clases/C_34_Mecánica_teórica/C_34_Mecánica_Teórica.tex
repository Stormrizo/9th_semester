\section{Lagrangiana de la clase anterior}

Tenemos: 

\[ L = L(q,\dot{q}, t)  \]

\[H(q,p,t) = p\dot{q} (q,p,t)  - L(q, \dot{q}(q,p,t),t) \]

\[ p \equiv \frac{ \partial L }{ \partial \dot{q} } \quad \Rightarrow \quad \dot{q} = \dot{q} (q,p,t)  \]


\[dH = \frac{\partial H}{\partial q}  dq + \frac{\partial H}{\partial p}dp+\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}dt\]


Tenemos:

\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
dH &= pd\dot{q} + \dot{q}dp-\frac{\partial L}{\partial q}dq - \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}}d\dot{q}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial t}dt \\
&= \left[ p - \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}} \right]d\dot{q} - \frac{\partial L}{\partial q}dq + \dot{q}dp - \frac{\partial L}{\partial t}dt
\end{split}
\end{equation}


Tenemos:



\[ dH = - \frac{ \partial L}{\partial q}  dq + \dot{q} dp - \frac{\partial L}{\partial t}dt \]

\[  \frac{d}{dt} \left( \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}}  \right) = \frac{ \partial L }{\partial q}  \]

\[ \dot{p} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial q}  \]


\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 dH  &= - \dot{p} dq + \dot{q} dp-\frac{\partial L}{\partial t}dt\\
 dH &= \frac{\partial H}{\partial q} dq + \frac{\partial H}{\partial p}dp + \frac{\partial H}{\partial t} dt
\end{split}
\end{equation}

\[ \boxed{\dot{p} = - \frac{  \partial H }{\partial q}, \quad \dot{q} = \frac{\partial H}{\partial p}    } \quad \leftarrow \quad \text{ecuaciones de Hamilton; 2 ecuaciones diferenciales de $1^{er}$ orden} \]

Oro punto importante es: 

\[ \boxed{- \frac{\partial L}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial H}{\partial t}}  \]

SI tenemos un lagrangiano que no depende explicitamente del tiempo, su función $H$ asociada es una constante de moviemiento que a veces coincide con la energía total del sistema.

\[ \boxed{ \color{Cinnabar} \frac{d}{dt} \left( \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial q} \right)   }  \quad \leftarrow \quad \text{Ecuación diferencial de $2^{do}$ orden para $q$, 2 C.I.}\]

\subsection{Ejemplo}


Tenemos la lagrangiana siguiente:


\[  L = \frac{1}{2} m \dot{x}^{2} \quad \overbrace{\to}^{T. Legendre} \quad H = \frac{p^{2}}{2m} \]

Tenemos:

\[\frac{d}{dt} \left(  \frac{ \partial L }{\partial \dot{x}} \right)- \frac{\partial L}{\partial x}   =0\]

\[ m\ddot{x} =0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x(t) = x_{0}+v_{0}t\]

\[ p = m\dot{x}  \]


\[ v_{0} = \frac{p_{0}}{m} \]


Tenemos


\[ H = \frac{p^{2} }{2m}  \]


\[ \boxed{\dot{p} = - \frac{ \partial H }{\partial q}  =0 } \]

\[   \boxed{ \color{Apple Green} \dot{x} = \frac{\partial H}{\partial p} = \frac{p}{m}  }  \]



\[  \boxed{ \color{Cerulean} \ddot{x} = \frac{\dot{p}}{m} =0  } \]

\[ \color{Apple Green} p(t) = p_{0} \]


\[ \dot{x} = \frac{ p_{0} }{m} \quad \Rightarrow \quad x(t) = x_{0} + \frac{p_{0}}{m}t \]

\subsection{Segundo ejercicio}


Tenemos la siguiente lagrangiana:


\[L = \frac{1}{2} m\dot{x}^{2} - \frac{1}{2} kx^{2}    \]


\[\frac{d}{dt} \left( \frac{\partial L  }{\partial \dot{x}} \right) - \frac{\partial L}{\partial x}  =0 \]


\[  m\ddot{x} + kx =0    \]


\[  \ddot{x} + \omega^{2}x =0 \]

\[ \omega^{2} \equiv \frac{k}{m} \]

\[ \boxed{x(t) = x_{0} \cos(\omega{t}) + \frac{v_{0} }{\omega} \sen(\omega{t})    } \]

\[ x(t) = a\cos(\omega{t}) + b\sen(\omega{t}) \]

\[ x(0) = x_{0}=a \]


\[ \dot{x} = -a\omega \sen(\omega{t}) + b\omega \cos(\omega{t}) \]


\[ \dot{x} (0) = v_{0} = b\omega \quad \Rightarrow \quad b = \frac{v_{0}}{\omega} \]

% --- Lagrangiano y momento canónico ---
\section*{Legendre \texorpdfstring{$(L\to H)$}{(L->H)} para el oscilador armónico}

\[
L(x,\dot{x})=\frac{1}{2}m\dot{x}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}k x^{2}, 
\qquad
p \equiv \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{x}}=m\dot{x}.
\]

% --- Transformada de Legendre ---
\subsection*{Hamiltoniano}
La transformada de Legendre está dada por
\[
H(x,p)=p\,\dot{x}-L(x,\dot{x})\quad\text{con}\quad \dot{x}=\dot{x}(x,p)=\frac{p}{m}.
\]
Sustituyendo:
\[
H(x,p)=p\left(\frac{p}{m}\right)-\left[\frac{1}{2}m\left(\frac{p}{m}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}k x^{2}\right]
=\frac{p^{2}}{m}-\left(\frac{p^{2}}{2m}-\frac{1}{2}k x^{2}\right)
=\boxed{\,\frac{p^{2}}{2m}+\frac{1}{2}k x^{2}\,}.
\]

% --- Identidad diferencial y ecuaciones de Hamilton ---
\subsection*{Identidad diferencial y ecuaciones de Hamilton}
Partiendo de
\[
dH=d(p\dot{x})-dL=\dot{x}\,dp+p\,d\dot{x}-
\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial x}\,dx+\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{x}}\,d\dot{x}\right),
\]
y usando \(p=\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{x}}\), se cancela \(p\,d\dot{x}\):
\[
dH=\dot{x}\,dp-\frac{\partial L}{\partial x}\,dx.
\]
Como \(dH=\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}dx+\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}dp\), identificamos
\[
\boxed{\;\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}=\dot{x}\;},\qquad
\boxed{\;\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}=-\frac{\partial L}{\partial x}\;}.
\]
Para nuestro \(L\), \(\frac{\partial L}{\partial x}=-kx\), de modo que
\[
\boxed{\;\dot{x}=\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}=\frac{p}{m}\;},\qquad
\boxed{\;\dot{p}=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}=-(-kx)=-kx\;}.
\]

% --- Ecuaciones de movimiento ---
\subsection*{Ecuación de movimiento y solución}
Derivando \(\dot{x}=\frac{p}{m}\):
\[
\ddot{x}=\frac{\dot{p}}{m}=-\frac{k}{m}x \;\;\Rightarrow\;\;
\boxed{\,\ddot{x}+\omega^{2}x=0\,},\qquad \omega\equiv\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}.
\]
Solución general:
\[
x(t)=A\cos(\omega t)+B\sin(\omega t),\qquad
p(t)=m\dot{x}(t)=-m\omega A\sin(\omega t)+m\omega B\cos(\omega t).
\]

% --- Conservación de la energía ---
\subsection*{Conservación de la energía}
El Hamiltoniano no depende explícitamente del tiempo, por lo que es constante:
\[
\frac{dH}{dt}=\{H,H\}+\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}=0.
\]
Explícitamente,
\[
H=\frac{p^{2}}{2m}+\frac{1}{2}k x^{2}=\text{constante}.
\]

\subsection*{Forma matricial (para usar $e^{Mt}$)}
Definiendo \( \mathbf{y}=(x,p)^{T}\), el sistema es
\[
\dot{\mathbf{y}}=M\,\mathbf{y},\qquad
M=
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & \tfrac{1}{m}\\[2pt]
-\,k & 0
\end{pmatrix},
\]
cuyo exponencial se puede escribir como
\[
e^{Mt}=
\cos(\omega t)\,\mathbb{I}+\frac{\sin(\omega t)}{\omega}\,M.
\]



Tenemos:

Nosotros queremos calcular:


\[ e^{Mt} = \sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(Mt)^{n} }{n!} = \mathbb{I} + \frac{Mt}{1!} + \frac{M^{2}t^{2}}{2!} + \frac{ M^{3}t^{3} }{3!} + \frac{M^{4}t^{4}}{4!} + \ldots \]

Desarrollando toda la expansión tenemos:

\[ e^{Mt} = \sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(Mt)^{n} }{n!} = \mathbb{I} + \frac{Mt}{1!} + \frac{M^{2}t^{2}}{2!} + \frac{ M^{3}t^{3} }{3!} + \frac{M^{4}t^{4}}{4!} + \ldots \]


\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}

\begin{aligned}

M^5&=M^4\,M=\left(-\frac{k}{m}\right)^{\!2}M=+\omega^4\,M,\\

\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
\]
