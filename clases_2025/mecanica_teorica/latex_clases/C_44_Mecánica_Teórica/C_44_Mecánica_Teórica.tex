Tenemos:

\[ vec{B} = B_{x}\hat{x}+B_{y}\hat{y}+B_{z}\hat{z} \]


Tenemos:


\[ \vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = A_{x}B_{x}+A_{y}B_{y}+A_{z}B_{z}  \]

Tenemos:


\[  = A_{1}B_{1}+ A_{2}B_{2} +A_{3}B_{3} \]


\[ = \sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}  A_{i}B_{i} = A_{i}B_{i}  \]


Tenemos:


\[  \vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = A_{i}B_{i}, \quad i=1.2.3 \]


Tenemos:


\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\vec{A} \times \vec{B}  &= \left| \begin{array}{ccc}
\hat{x} & \hat{y} & \hat{z}\\
A_{x} & A_{y} & A_{z}\\
B_{x} & B_{y} & B_{z}
\end{array}\right| \\
&= \hat{x} (A_{y}B_{z}-A_{z}B_{y}) - \hat{y} (A_{x}B_{z}-A_{z}B_{x}) + \hat{z} (A_{x}B_{y}-A_{y}B_{x})
\end{split}
\end{equation}

Entonces

Cada vez que aparecen dos índices repetidos existe una sumatoria.

Tenemos:


\[ L_{i} = (\vec{r} \times \vec{p})_{i} = \epsilon_{is\ell} \times_{s}p_{\ell} \]


\[  L_{j} = (\vec{r}\times \vec{p})_{j} = \epsilon_{jnm} \times_{n}p_{m}  \]


Tenemos:


\[  \left[  L_{i}, L_{j}  \right] = \left[  \epsilon_{is\ell} \times_{s}p_{\ell}, \epsilon_{jnm}\times_{n}p_{m}  \right] \]

\[ = \epsilon_{is\ell}\epsilon_{jnm} \left[ \times_{s} p_{\ell}, \times_{n}p_{m}  \right] \]


Tenemos:



\[ = \epsilon_{is\ell} \epsilon_{jnm} \left\{  \times_{n} \left[ \times_{s}p_{\ell}, p_{m}  \right] + \left[ \times_{s}p_{\ell}, \times_{n}  \right] p_{m}  \right\} \]



\[ \epsilon_{is\ell} \epsilon_{jnm} \left\{   \times_{n}\times_{s}\underbrace{\left[  p_{\ell}, p_{m}  \right]}_{=0} + \times x_{n} \underbrace{\left[ \times_{s},p_{m}  \right]}_{=\delta_{sm}} p_{\ell} + \times_{s} \underbrace{\left[  p_{\ell}, \times_{n} \right]}_{=-\delta_{\ell n}}p_{m} + \underbrace{\left[ x_{s}, x_{n}  \right]}_{=0} p_{\ell}p_{m} \right\}  \]
